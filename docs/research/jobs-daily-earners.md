# Daily and weekly wage earners (Phase 1 research)

Evidence note: figures come from web-search result summaries; I did not open the full BBS reports. "Assumption" marks anything unsourced.

## 1. Day labourers (construction, farm, porters)

**Size.** About 84.9% of working Bangladeshis were in informal jobs in the 2022 Labour Force Survey ([Bonik Barta](https://en.bonikbarta.com/bangladesh/WyzU66qZ4d2xHfDN)); agriculture is about 97% informal ([Observer, via ILO analysis](https://observerbd.com/news/588021)). A 2024 count (58.04 million informal of 69.10 million) is in [Financial Express](https://thefinancialexpress.com.bd/views/columns/the-urgency-of-recognising-informal-workforce); the Observer cites a conflicting 80.8%, so use the 2022 figure. No source found for the headcount of day labourers alone.

**Income pattern.** Daily pay, uneven days. Farm pay swings with season (an anecdotal Sirajganj farm labourer: Tk 300 a day, Tk 700 at paddy harvest) and one Dhaka construction worker reported Tk 650 a day ([Daily Star](https://images.thedailystar.net/node/3524871); I could not confirm the exact article). A gazette reportedly sets construction pay at Tk 1,020 (city) and Tk 940 (village), but the date is unclear ([Daily Star](https://www.thedailystar.net/node/3362136)). These are individual accounts, not averages. Wage growth for low-paid workers has trailed inflation since 2022 ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/business/local/8o7nz99m12)).

**Money in/out.** In: cash from the contractor or sardar, often paid weekly (assumption). Out: food, house rent, fares, remittance to family, medicine. Channels: cash mostly; bKash for sending home (assumption); baki at the local tea or grocery shop; samity/NGO instalments (assumption, no source found).

**Habits, literacy, phone.** No source found. Assumption: memory plus a small notebook kept by the shop or contractor; many have only a feature or basic Android phone. GSMA 2024: 85% of men own a mobile but only about 40% use mobile internet ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/science-technology/it/xpffxivs1x)).

**Pain points (assumption).** Idle days with no income; unpaid or late wages; baki that grows unnoticed; no record to dispute a contractor.

**Top 3 logs.**
1. Day's earning, with a one-tap "worked today" and a default wage (assumption).
2. Baki taken or repaid at a shop, via a named-person quick-add.
3. Money sent home, logged by voice or a preset amount.

## 2. Rickshaw pullers and CNG/auto/battery drivers

**Size.** Dhaka has had roughly a million pedal rickshaws for years ([Dhaka Tribune](https://www.dhakatribune.com/bangladesh/dhaka/380990/is-a-rickshaw-free-dhaka-really-possible)). BRTA reportedly estimates over 6 million battery-run rickshaws nationally, 1 to 1.2 million in Dhaka; independent estimates vary widely ([Bonik Barta](https://en.bonikbarta.com/bangladesh/Lhv57slcn0748lpc/), [Asia News Network](https://asianews.network/?p=306141)). There is no official count.

**Income pattern.** Daily. A BILS survey (2019, 200 pullers) found Tk 13,382 a month, with 96% renting at about Tk 111 a day ([Financial Express](https://today.thefinancialexpress.com.bd/print/a-city-rickshaw-puller-earns-tk-13382-a-month-1561654127)). Covid-era (2020): about Tk 513 net a day ([Innovision](https://innovision-bd.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/COVID-19_Digest-1_Rickshawpullers.pdf)). Battery rickshaws: a garage owner charged about Tk 350 a day rent and Tk 40-50 for charging ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/bangla-tesla-owners-and-their-quest-liberty-labour-864976)); a Daily Star analysis puts garage rent near half of gross income ([Daily Star](https://online91.thedailystar.net/slow-reads/unheard-voices/news/dhakas-tesla-technological-upgrade-or-poverty-trap-4144236); not opened, snippet only). Anecdotal figures, not survey averages.

**Money in/out.** In: cash fares, all day. Out: daily joma to the garage owner, charging, repairs, food, house rent, loan instalment if the vehicle is bought on credit, and informal payments. Channels: cash; bKash mainly for remittances (assumption); baki at tea stalls (assumption).

**Habits, literacy, phone.** No source found. Assumption: mental accounting at day's end, with joma first and the rest as "what is left". Mixed phone types.

**Pain points.** Joma is owed on slow days; rising repair and charging cost; policy risk of battery-rickshaw restrictions ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/bangladesh/tougher-policy-soon-regulate-battery-run-rickshaws-bangladesh-1207206)).

**Top 3 logs.**
1. Fare total at day's end, via a big-number keypad or quick-amount buttons (assumption).
2. Fixed daily costs (joma, charging) with one tap on a saved preset.
3. Repair or spare-part spend, by voice.

## 3. Ready-made garment (RMG) workers

**Size.** No current headcount found from a reliable source. Minimum entry wage is Tk 12,500 from 1 Dec 2023 (up from Tk 8,000 in 2018), with 5% annual increments ([MiHCM](https://mihcm.com/resources/blog/payroll-for-the-ready-made-garment-industry-in-bangladesh-managing-complexity-at-scale/); vendor blog, not the gazette).

**Income pattern.** Monthly wage plus overtime, paid on a roughly monthly cycle with Eid bonuses (assumption on bonuses). One survey found 82% were paid digitally in May 2020 ([TBS](https://www.tbsnews.net/economy/rmg/82-garment-workers-paid-digitally-may-survey-84367)). The payroll blog says many are still paid in cash.

**Money in/out.** In: bank, bKash, or cash. After digital pay, most workers withdraw all or nearly all in cash at an ATM or agent ([BIGD](https://bigd.bracu.ac.bd/cash-or-bytes-garment-workers-payment-preferences/)). In an 84-worker 2020 survey, 91% sent money to the village monthly, and 80% had no savings ([Innovision](https://innovision-bd.com/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/COVID-19-RMG-Workers.pdf)). A 115-worker study found 41.7% with long-term debt ([Innovision](https://innovision-bd.com/reports/digest-3-deep-dive-on-the-rmg-workers-of-bangladesh-in-selected-clusters)). Outflows: rent, food, remittance, loans. Baki at the shop and samity instalments are an assumption.

**Habits, literacy, phone.** No reliable source found. Women lag in mobile ownership: 68% vs 85% for men (GSMA 2024, [Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/science-technology/it/xpffxivs1x)), so many may use shared or basic phones (assumption).

**Pain points.** Overtime and deduction disputes (assumption); cash-out fees; no savings buffer; no record of remittances.

**Top 3 logs.**
1. Salary received, with a prompt on payday and a split between bKash and cash.
2. Remittance sent to the village, repeat the last amount in one tap.
3. Rent and grocery baki, by preset category.

## Common patterns across the group

- Cash dominates daily life, with bKash used for sending money home.
- Income is lumpy. Fixed outflows (joma, rent, remittance) come first, so the useful number is "what is left".
- Baki and dena-pawna are tracked by memory (assumption), so a person-wise ledger fits.
- Feature phones, low literacy and Bangla numerals are plausible but not sourced. Design for big keypads, presets, voice and Bangla digits (assumption).
- Real wages are squeezed: wage growth has lagged inflation ([Prothom Alo](https://en.prothomalo.com/business/local/8o7nz99m12)).
